## 2. Independent mathematical derivation

Use a local frame with the ideal plane at x=0, an object at (-d,0) in index n_o, and a receiving half-space of index n_v. Let r=n_o/n_v. Opposite object-medium incident angles ±i strike the interface at (0,±d tan i). For a transmitting branch, Snell gives sin(t)=r sin(i); outgoing line extensions obey y=±(d tan i+x tan t). Their *backward* intersection lies at x=-d tan(i)/tan(t), y=0. Thus the two-ray normal depth is

**d_s(i) = d tan(i)/tan(t) = (d/r) sqrt(1-r² sin²(i))/cos(i).**

Its near-normal limit is **d_a=d/r=d n_v/n_o**, not an independently native-observed physical image. With z=sin²(i),

**(d_s/d_a)²=(1-r²z)/(1-z), and d/dz[(d_s/d_a)²]=(1-r²)/(1-z)².**

This proves endpoint monotonicity across a transmitting cone: for r>1 the symmetric-pair depth decreases as i increases, for r<1 it increases, and r=1 gives exactly d. Computing via the square-root formula or a direct equal-index branch avoids near-zero tangent division. No general observer, pupil, physical-image uniqueness, or accuracy theorem follows from this conditional symmetric-pair construction.

For the 1.5 → 1 control the critical angle is asin(2/3)=**41.81031489578°**. Under the stated [1,4] index bounds, the *maximum* admitted object-side Snell sine is 4sin(5°)=**0.34862297099**, far below one, and cos(5°)=**0.99619469809**. The admitted cone is therefore safely away from both critical and grazing incidence. The 1 → 1.5, 89.999° mathematical pair gives approximately **7,687,035.47 mm** symmetric depth for d=120 mm, illustrating why the new grazing/wide-cone refusal is necessary even without TIR. A supplied 0°-to-5° maximum still must satisfy the 1% model-construction discrepancy; a missing cone cannot be replaced with an inferred quantitative limit.

The **5° and 1% values are justified as an explicit bounded educational product admission policy**, not universal paraxial physics tolerances. The 1% comparison is solely between the paraxial limit and the specified extreme symmetric two-ray model; it is **not** a bound on an actual viewer's apparent-point error, native numerical error, or non-symmetric rays.
